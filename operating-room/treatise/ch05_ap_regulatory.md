# V. The legal road — recognition, not relief

American Perpetual's legal strategy asks for no new law. A single-stock perpetual is a contract for future delivery of one security, so by statute it is a "security future". The statute also excludes every security futures product from the definition of "swap". The path to listing is therefore the ordinary security-futures path, run in a fixed order:

1. OCC agrees.
2. A recognition letter goes to both Commission Chairmen.
3. Three filings go in on the same day.

Every filing in the data room is fully drafted. As of the September 30, 2026 compilation, none had been filed.

> [!quote] "MIAX Futures therefore seeks recognition, not relief" — Harmonization Submission (draft), Sept 2026

MIAX Futures is the sole petitioner of record. AP drafted the package but "prepares and files no rule, submission or approval and presents nothing."

## The posture: no exemption, no no-action, no pilot {#apreg-posture}

The premise is that the product complies with the rules as written. The Harmonization Submission lists four things it does not request:

- **No exemptive relief.** Cash settlement, 15% margin, position limits, surveillance, Section 31 and tax are "each the existing rule applied". Terminal settlement is compliance under 17 CFR 41.25(c)(2)(i) and (c)(3), "not an exemption from anything."
- **No no-action position.** "MIAX Futures seeks recognition that the product is what it is, not an assurance that the staff will decline to recommend enforcement."
- **No new product category and no rulemaking precondition.**
- **No pilot or innovation exemption.**

What it does expect are the ordinary filings any new contract needs:

- MIAX's Part 41 certifications;
- Form 19b-7;
- a Form 19b-4 for its margin rule;
- OCC's clearing-rule change;
- the FINRA/NFA disclosure supplement.

"None is an exemption from the security-futures framework; each is that framework, applied."

> [!quote] "The product does not need a new lane built for it, and it does not need relief to occupy the one it is in; it needs recognition that it already occupies an existing lane." — Harmonization Submission (draft), Sept 2026

This posture is the main contrast with CME. CME relaunched dated single-stock futures on conditional, revocable relief, with conditions: mega-caps only, contracts of 9 months or less, daily position reporting, a mandated market-impact study, and that exchange alone.

*Sources: Harmonization Submission, Parts VIII–IX (draft, Sept 2026); MIAX Brief End-to-End, §§03, 08 (Sept 2026); Runbooks v1.9, R1 (Sep 17, 2026; rev. Sep 30, 2026)*

## The legal theory: futurity first, then the exclusion {#apreg-theory}

The argument has two steps, and their order matters. Futurity puts the instrument in the security-future category. The statutory exclusion then keeps it clear of every swap question.

**Step 1: it is a security future.** CEA §1a(44) and Exchange Act §3(a)(55)(A) define a "security future" as "a contract of sale for future delivery of a single security or of a narrow-based security index". A security futures product (SFP) is a security future or an option on one (§1a(45)). The CFMA of 2000 put the category under joint SEC/CFTC jurisdiction. The Form 19b-7 and the rulebook also cite CEA §1a(27) as the source of "future delivery". The documents cite different paragraphs, but the point is the same.

**Step 2: therefore it is not a swap.** CEA §1a(47)(B)(i) expressly excludes any "security futures product" from "swap". The exclusion carries into "security-based swap" too. Exchange Act §3(a)(68)(A)(i) adopts the swap definition "without regard to paragraph (47)(B)(x)", which switches off only clause (x) and leaves (B)(i) in force. An SFP is therefore neither a swap nor a security-based swap. The swap-versus-future dispute being litigated over commodity perpetuals "does not reach a security futures product."

### "Future delivery" is convergence, not a calendar

Futurity is the only contested element. The Submission reads it functionally. A cash-settled future delivers nothing, so "future delivery" must mean a price tethered to, and made to converge on, an underlying reference. An expiry date is one way to get convergence. Funding is another.

> [!quote] "The perpetual future achieves the same convergence continuously rather than terminally." — Harmonization Submission (draft), Sept 2026

Each hour, the funding rate equals the average of (mark − index)/index divided by 24. When the contract trades rich, longs pay; when it trades cheap, shorts pay. This is "precisely the arbitrage pressure that collapses basis at a dated expiry, applied every hour instead of once." The Submission claims the perpetual shows convergence "more cleanly than a dated future".

The cases cited:

- **The Kalshi Order** (CFTC, May 29, 2026), treated as persuasive.
- **CME v. SEC** (7th Cir. 1989), **CFTC v. Erskine** (6th Cir. 2008) and **CFTC v. Standard Forex** (E.D.N.Y. 1996). These locate a future in standardized, exchange-traded, centrally cleared price-risk transfer with convergence to a reference, not in physical delivery.
- **CFTC v. Zelener** (7th Cir. 2004), distinguished as rolling spot FX with no clearinghouse and no convergence.

### The observability bridge

The CFTC's perpetuals policy statement requires a reference "reliable at every funding interval, without interruption, for as long as the contract remains active." The Submission answers that a listed U.S. equity, referenced to the SIP consolidated NBBO, is "the paradigm of a deep, active, continuous, and reliable reference". The binding price, the 4:00 p.m. settlement, forms in the deepest market of the day. Reliability comes from exclusion, not estimation:

- a sample counts only if the quote is two-sided and not crossed;
- loss of the reference pauses funding;
- permanent loss triggers a Terminal Settlement Event.

> [!quote] "Exclusion, not estimation, is the reliability guarantee." — Harmonization Submission (draft), Sept 2026

Funding is variation, not a bilateral swap payment stream. The Exchange cannot compute per-member cash, because after novation only OCC sees members' netted positions: "An 'exchange-computes-the-cash' design is therefore structurally foreclosed, not merely disfavored."

> [!tension] CME v. Selig (D.D.C., filed June 18, 2026) asks a court to treat funding-rate perpetuals as swaps; the Submission argues the SFP exclusion keeps this product outside that fight, and the suit is undecided (the CFTC moved to dismiss).

## The Kalshi order: persuasive, not controlling {#apreg-kalshi}

AP's theory leans on a precedent set four months before the data room closed. On May 28, 2026, KalshiEX LLC submitted a bitcoin perpetual (BTCPERP) under CFTC Regulation 40.3. On May 29, 2026, the CFTC approved it as a futures contract under CEA §5c(c)(4) (Press Release 9240-26). It issued a policy statement on perpetual contracts at the same time. The contract references CF Benchmarks' Bitcoin Real Time Index.

The Submission takes three things from the order:

1. **The holding.** Funding "incentivizes the futures contract's price to converge to the spot market price", and "[t]hat terminal convergence is why a futures contract is a futures contract."
2. **The limit.** The order covers only perpetuals on "bitcoin or other digital commodities … that have deep, active, and continuous spot market trading."
3. **The invitation.** Perpetuals on other asset classes are directed to Regulation 40.3 review, case by case.

MIAX cites the order as persuasive reasoning on "future delivery", not as controlling authority. Its scope is limited to digital commodities, and it is in litigation. The equity case, the Submission argues, is cleaner, because the swap exclusion removes "the very classification dispute being litigated for commodity perpetuals."

> [!quote] "The single-name equity perpetual is the natural next case, and the cleanest one." — Harmonization Submission (draft), Sept 2026

The order moved markets. The Deal says the first crypto-perpetual approval took 9% off Cboe in a session and about 4% off CME and ICE, and it dates that reaction to June 2, 2026. How to Win dates the approval to May 29. The room does not reconcile the two dates; the reaction may simply have come in later sessions.

Kalshi kept moving after the order. Its gold and silver perpetuals went live around the clock on September 10, 2026. On September 18 it filed perpetual SFPs on 58 names.

### Eligibility: the six conditions

Rule 43.2(a) restates the security-futures standards as six conjunctive conditions, all judged from public information:

1. An eligible class under SEC Rel. 34-61027 (Nov 19, 2009), which means any options-eligible security; plus Section 12 registration and reporting compliance, with a 30-day cure.
2. An NMS stock listed on a national securities exchange.
3. At least 7,000,000 publicly held shares, excluding Section 16(a) holders.
4. At least 2,000 holders.
5. At least 27,500,000 shares of volume over 12 months.
6. A lowest close of at least $5.00 over 5 business days.

Against Cboe's options listing guideline, the volume test is "more restrictive, by more than eleven times": 27.5 million shares against 2.4 million. A name that stops qualifying goes closing-only by Corporate Action Alert and may be withdrawn.

**No. 101 is an Affiliate Security.** MIAX listing its own parent's stock requires:

- a Market Regulation determination;
- Regulatory Oversight Committee approval before listing, from a committee of at least three Public Directors;
- quarterly reports;
- an annual independent accounting-firm review;
- written notice to both Commissions within 5 Business Days of any noncompliance.

The models are NYSE Rule 497, NYSE Texas Rule 24, Nasdaq Rule 4370 and Cboe BZX Rule 14.3(e).

The other §41.22 certifications rest on existing facts:

- dual trading is satisfied by an electronic platform with no floor and no override;
- manipulation is contained by the SIP midpoint, the median Mark, the collar and the hour-long average;
- surveillance is ISG coverage of each name, a listing condition under Rule 2.1.18.

## The SEC side: Form 19b-7, Form 19b-4 and Section 6(g) {#apreg-sec}

The SEC side has two filings and one open question. Form 19b-7 takes effect on the CFTC's action. Form 19b-4 runs on its own clock, and the first day of trading does not wait for it.

### Form 19b-7: the listing rules

The 16-page Form 19b-7 is filed once, through EFFS, under Exchange Act §19(b)(7). It adopts:

- the Rule 43.2 listing standards;
- the customer-protection rules (2.1.14 G on sales practices, 2.1.18 on the joint framework and ISG, 2.1.19 on risk-disclosure delivery);
- Chapter 43 and its conforming amendments, including new Rule 6.21, the public funding endpoint.

It takes effect under §19(b)(7)(B) upon the CFTC's §41.23(b) approval. The SEC then has 60 days to summarily abrogate it. Later position-limit changes, including the conversion of Nos. 35, 39 and 77 at their first reviews, go to the CFTC under §41.24 only, with no new 19b-7.

The form also answers Exchange Act §6(h)(3) clause by clause, from (A) to (L), following OneChicago's 2003 analysis. Clause (E), linked clearing, remains deferred under §6(h)(7).

### Form 19b-4: the customer-margin rule

The 11-page Form 19b-4 asks for SEC approval under §19(b)(2) of new **Rule 3.3.2, Customer Margin for Security Futures Products**. It has to be a separate filing. The federal regulations allow offsets, the portfolio-margin exclusion and the dealer exclusion only through SRO rules "effective in accordance with section 19(b)(2)".

The rule:

- sets margin on OCC scan ranges, never below 15% of current market value, except for offsets and portfolio-margin accounts;
- adopts the 2020 Strategy-Based Offset Table (85 FR 75112);
- creates **Security Futures Dealers**, Market Participants who hold themselves out with continuous two-sided quotes under the Market Maker Program, within a maximum width and a minimum share of the session. This is the analog of OneChicago Rule 515(n). Their positions are margined on OCC clearing-level parameters rather than customer margin. They register on a new Chapter 70 form.

The models are OneChicago SR-OC-2002-01 (2002), CME SR-CME-2026-001 (approved July 13, 2026) and Coinbase Derivatives SR-COIN-2026-001. The SEC has 45 days from Federal Register publication, extendable to 90. MIAX **does not consent** to the extension. The product can list before the rule is approved. Until then the federal rules apply directly and nobody takes the dealer exclusion. Market makers register as Security Futures Dealers on the day of approval (link 59).

> [!tension] MIAX does not object to a CFTC §40.5 extension but does not consent to an SEC §19(b)(2) extension, so the two clocks are treated differently on purpose.

## Risk disclosure: one ask of FINRA {#apreg-disclosure}

Customer sales wait on one document that neither MIAX nor AP writes. FINRA and NFA must supplement the uniform Security Futures Risk Disclosure Statement. The 4-page Risk Disclosure Supplement Brief hands the drafters every product fact, section by section, and makes one ask.

> [!quote] "The one document FINRA and NFA must write — and everything they need to write it." — Risk Disclosure Supplement Brief, Sept 2026

The Statement was approved by the SEC in 2002. Its current form is the 2020 Statement and Supplement (FINRA Regulatory Notice 20-28; implemented September 14, 2020). It describes only dated contracts. The nearest precedent is the 2010 supplement's dividend paragraph, written for OneChicago NoDivRisk: "this product's own ancestor."

**Three product facts for retail:**

1. **No expiration.** A position lasts until it is offset or a Terminal Settlement Event occurs, and every ending is in cash.
2. **Funding.** It is charged hourly, in either direction, with no cap. A locked rate is final. Funding nets to zero, settles daily, and "is not a fee." A position held at a lock bears the whole hour. A position opened and closed between locks bears none. A partly halted hour settles a full hour, computed from its live samples.
3. **Endings.** A contract can end by corporate-action termination, by market-loss termination (including a halt of ten consecutive trading days), by a closing-only restriction, or by withdrawal.

The changes to the Statement are few. §2.6.2 on expiration becomes inapplicable. The margin section must "state both" levels, because the Statement still says 20%. "Everything else in the Statement holds for this product exactly as written."

**The ask: FINRA Rule 4210, from 20% to 15%.** The federal floor fell to 15% on December 24, 2020. FINRA's Rule 4210(c)(5) and (f)(10) still hold security futures at 20% in a securities account. A non-portfolio-margined securities customer therefore "posts a third more than a futures-account customer for the same position." The room asks FINRA to conform.

The sales-practice overlay is already drafted as Rule 2.1.14 G.4. A recommendation must disclose three things: no expiration, hourly accrual with daily settlement, and termination on a Terminal Settlement Event. A new account-approval form adds a checkbox for "no-expiration, funding-bearing security futures."

> A non-portfolio-margined securities customer "posts a third more"; FINRA is asked to conform Rule 4210 to the federal floor.

## Surveillance through ISG {#apreg-isg}

Surveillance runs on credentials MIAX already holds. Its existing Intermarket Surveillance Group membership covers each Reference Security (Rule 2.1.18; §41.22(g); Exchange Act §6(h)(3)(I)). Nothing is asked of ISG as a body: "no filing, no approval, no fee, no new agreement."

The 13-page ISG Surveillance Brief maps five numbers that move money:

- the Index;
- the Mark;
- the Funding Rate;
- the Daily Settlement Price;
- news, covered by Rule 43.23's ban on trading by issuer insiders and holders of material non-public information.

Each number is kept as a 5-second series. The manipulation map names "the funding push, marking the settlement window, moving the Index in thin hours". The thin hours are 9:00 p.m.–9:30 a.m. and 4:00–8:00 p.m. The Brief prices the push: at a $180.00 Mark, a 1-basis-point hourly rate requires a 0.24% average premium sustained for the hour, and moves $1.80 per contract.

Because every lock and every settlement is final, surveillance never asks for a price to be recomputed.

> [!quote] "the surveillance question in this class is never 're-run the number'; it is always 'who did it, and in what accounts'." — ISG Surveillance Brief, Sept 2026

Halts follow the stock automatically. "What the stock's listing market decides, the contract follows within the same five-second sample." Records join on Instrument ID, symbol, SIP ticker and a nanosecond clock. Three things are confirmed before listing:

- the ISG membership tier and its single-equity data coverage;
- coverage of each name before it is certified;
- a named representative.

At T−1 a one-page ISG notice with 11 fields goes out (link 53).

> [!tension] The Brief's own push example "does not establish an attack-cost bound", and lock-timing behaviour (enter just after a lock, exit just before) is not on its vector list.

## OCC's own rule filing {#apreg-occ}

OCC files one ordinary rule change, with the SEC under Rule 19b-4 and with the CFTC under Part 40. It has three parts:

1. **A Terminal-Settlement standing rule** under 17 CFR 41.25(c)(3) and SEC Rule 6h-1(b)(3). It applies three statutory factors: fairness to buyers and sellers, a fair and orderly market, and consistent interpretation.
2. **An express provision** that the class adjusts under Rule 2803. Rule 2804(a) otherwise governs cash-settled contracts "except as expressly provided otherwise".
3. **The clearing-agreement amendment.** The May 20, 2026 agreement (SR-OCC-2025-008) reaches only futures "other than security futures".

The By-Laws, the default waterfall and the recovery-and-wind-down plan need no change. The filing "gates clearing go-live, not the listing."
