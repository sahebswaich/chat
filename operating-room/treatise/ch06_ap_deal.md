# VI. The deal — sixty percent, held by paying

The deal is one master license with two numbers. American Perpetual LLC licenses everything Saheb built for U.S. equity perpetual futures to MIAX Futures Exchange, LLC. In return MIAX pays 60% of the transaction fees it actually collects on every perpetual product it or an affiliate ever lists, on any underlying, with no end date. It also pays $40,000,000 at signing, straight to Saheb. MIAX can never terminate the agreement. It keeps its exclusivity only for as long as it keeps paying.

The final text is dated 30 September 2026 and is ready to sign. Nothing in the record shows that it was signed, and on 16 October Saheb's own notes still treated it as unsigned.

This chapter covers the company, the agreement, the money, the math, the history from Greyline's 20%, the value, the tax, counsel's flags and where things stand.

> [!number] 60% — of Covered Fees, flat, on every perpetual product
> Net only of the SEC's Section 31 assessment. No floor, no tiers, "never amended downward" (MPPLA ¶5(a)).

> [!number] $40,000,000 — signing payment, paid to Saheb personally
> Non-refundable, never credited against the royalty, outside every cap. The license takes effect only when he has received it (¶3(a), ¶5(h)).

## American Perpetual LLC — the rent, not the building {#deal-llc}

The company is a single-purpose box: it owns the work and receives the royalty, and MIAX bears every operating and regulatory cost.

> [!quote] "This is the economics of an exchange without the exchange." — Deal, Structure & Closing Memo, 8 Jun 2026

**Formation.** Organized in Delaware on 1 June 2026 (file no. 10643461) by Nat Smith of Northwest Registered Agent Service, Inc.; registered office 8 The Green, Suite B, Dover, DE 19901. EIN letter 1 June; good standing 5 June. The formation operating agreement is an unsigned template; the organizer's resolutions adopted only "general provisions of an operating agreement" (F-34).

**Ownership.** Saheb is the sole member. The September papers recapitalize the company into 100,000,000 Units (1,000,000 = 1%) for one secondary sale of 10%, leaving Saheb 90,000,000. The June version used 36,000,000 Units, one per "royalty point" of the fee pool.

**Governance after the amended and restated operating agreement (A&R OA).**

- Manager-managed, with Saheb as Manager and Partnership Representative.
- Investors get negative consents (no debt, no new units, no sale of the royalty), most-favored-nation terms, anti-dilution, tag-along and a company right of first offer.
- Saheb's own transfers are capped at 10,000,000 Units without investor consent (§5.5; 8,000,000 at v4.46).
- Provider fees are capped at the greater of $250,000 a year or 25 bps of royalty received (§6.8).
- Saheb carries the pre-royalty costs (§6.11) and may run other ventures, even competing ones (§9.2).

**Money flows through, never pools.**

- The $40M never touches the company; the master assigns that receivable to Saheb (¶5(h)).
- The royalty lands in a paying agent's collection account at a regulated bank or trust company (Computershare, Wilmington Trust or CSC class).
- An administrator (Carta, Waystone or CSC class) runs a fixed waterfall: withholding, a small reserve, capped fixed costs, then pro-rata distribution. A CPA files the returns.
- Saheb is nowhere in the cash path.

> [!tension] README_INSTRUMENTS says "the Company is member-managed: no Manager office"; the A&R OA and Investor Master make Saheb the Manager. Counsel must conform them (F-37).

Two smaller June-to-September mismatches: the Delaware annual tax ($300 vs $400) and distributions (monthly vs quarterly).

## The master agreement, clause by clause {#deal-mppla}

The master is written on Bloomberg Index Services' master-license form (public on EDGAR, the SEC's filing database), in the shape of MIAX's own Bloomberg index license. That makes it a commercial form MIAX has signed before. It departs from that form twice: it licenses a finished, delivered apparatus rather than an ongoing index service, and the royalty stream is "built to be owned."

### What MIAX gets

MIAX gets an exclusive license to the "Originator Work": "product architecture, the funding methodology, the mark, settlement, and terminal-settlement design, the corporate-actions framework, the rulebook drafting, the technical specifications, and the regulatory work product" (Recital A). It may list on its Onyx platform and its affiliates' venues.

A product counts as a Perpetual Product by what it does, not what it is called. Any one of three tests is enough (¶1):

1. It has no scheduled expiry, or one that rolls without a new trade.
2. It uses a periodic funding, carry, premium, interest or reset payment that holds its price to the underlying. A funding rate is the periodic payment between longs and shorts that ties the perpetual to the stock.
3. It is "designated, marketed or described as perpetual, continuous, rolling, open-ended" or an equivalent.

Options, spreads and strips with a perpetual leg are included. On venues a buyer of MIAX already ran, only contracts built on the Originator Work pay (added 28 Sept).

### The base: Covered Fees

Covered Fees are "the transaction fees the Exchange and its Affiliates actually collect on Perpetual Products… less any rebate, discount, waiver, or promotional credit actually given… net only of the assessment under Section 31." They are never below zero.

No MIAX cost reduces them. ¶1 names market-data, data-license and index-source fees expressly. Saheb asked for that in his own words: "any like fee/license for like market data is covered not passed through to me."

The anti-impairment clause (¶5(g)) bars diverting fees to another revenue line, migrating or mirroring volume to shrink the royalty, and rebates that are not bona fide, published and open to all.

### Term: perpetually bound

¶2 says "the Parties intend to be perpetually bound." The agreement never expires and never renews. Only American Perpetual can end it, and only in three cases:

- MIAX's uncured material breach: 30 days to cure, 15 for an undisputed payment (¶9(a));
- MIAX's insolvency (¶9(b));
- no first listing within five years of signing, "until something is" (¶2(d)).

Every ending runs off: the royalty continues until open interest (the contracts still outstanding) is wound down (¶9(d)). MIAX's remedies against AP are injunction, specific performance and capped damages, "never termination, rescission, suspension or withheld payment."

> [!quote] "im not going to fire miax, im not going to end the deal, i dont want them to be able to end it either." — Saheb, PREPACK v4.76, 28 Sep 2026

### Exclusivity, held by paying

Exclusivity covers U.S. DCMs and U.S. national securities exchanges. It starts only when Saheb receives the signing payment (¶3(a)). It releases to non-exclusive, with the license and the royalty unchanged, in two cases:

- nothing lists within 18 months of signing (the "Longstop"). Delays by MIAX, OCC or regulators never extend it;
- after launch, no royalty accrues for 12 consecutive calendar quarters.

The Investor Master calls the second test "three straight years." Non-U.S. venues of others, and non-exchange uses, stay free to AP. On 28 September Saheb also removed classes ("class concept does not exist"), so one exclusivity covers every perpetual product.

> [!number] 12 quarters — no royalty for three straight years and exclusivity lapses
> Exclusivity is held by paying, not by listing. The 18-month Longstop covers the time before the first listing.

### Launch cooperation: licensor, not staff

Saheb owes one bounded thing after delivery (¶4(c)): walkthroughs, agreed written clarifications, and factual explanations for MIAX's presentations to the SEC, CFTC, OCC and self-regulatory organizations. He gives them "reasonably… on notice," until the first listing and never beyond the Longstop. There is no pay and no hours cap, following the S&P–CME index-license form; MIAX's only remedy is specific performance.

He refused any employment role: "I won't be there for like 4 years to have everything vested. So like I don't want employment at all." The royalty does not depend on any of this.

> [!quote] "The Royalty follows the products, not the design." — MPPLA ¶4(b), 30 Sep 2026

### Protections around the stream

- **Payments absolute.** No set-off; audits up to twice a year; late interest WSJ prime + 2%; an underpayment over 2% puts the audit cost on MIAX; seven-year records (¶5(c), ¶5(e)).
- **Guaranty.** MIH guarantees both payments for amounts accruing while MIAX is in its group, through any bankruptcy, a rejection included (¶5(i)).
- **Assignment.** MIAX cannot assign without consent, and a merger is not an assignment. AP's side, and its equity, move freely (¶11). "Whoever owns MIAX, MIAX pays."
- **Confirmation.** Within 10 business days, up to twice a year, MIAX confirms in writing to a buyer or lender AP names that the agreement is in force and the royalty paid (¶18).
- **Credit line.** "American Perpetual LLC — originator and licensor," plus a disclaimer; MIAX may use the name in product names (¶6(d)). The May term sheet had gone further, with a class designation in the manner of "American" and "European" option styles.

> [!tension] Counsel's note N-2: an affiliate sold out of MIH's group "leaves the License, and its listings then pay nothing — the one gap left."

## The $40M — how the fixed cash moved {#deal-signing-payment}

The fixed cash went from $1M at signing plus monthly availability fees to a single $40M payment in two weeks (11 to 25 September). The royalty went up at the same time. Each step also removed things: availability fees, a services schedule, later-class prices.

| Date (cut) | Fixed cash | What sat beside it |
|---|---|---|
| Aug – 11 Sep | $1,000,000 at signing + $500,000 at first listing | $125,000/month founder availability (Launch Services Schedule) |
| 14 Sep (v4.4) | Not restated in the state note | Further months of availability only by a "Months Instrument" at $83,333.33 each; later classes AP delivers paid by the 35% rate alone |
| 18 Sep (Deal Sept ed.) | $5,000,000 at signing | $1,000,000 founder availability over 12 months, paid monthly in advance |
| 24 Sep (v4.39) | $6,000,000 at signing | Nothing. "no availability" |
| 24 Sep (v4.42) | $10,000,000 at signing | Nothing ("Change to 10m") |
| 25 Sep (v4.55) | $40,000,000 at signing | Nothing. Nothing at first listing |

The $6M equals the $5M plus the $1M of the 18 September edition, folded into one payment. Saheb's instruction for that cut was:

> [!quote] "60 and 6m, one royalty for all perpetuals. no availability hand over yeah agree, licensor not staff. Royalty no floor." — Saheb, PREPACK v4.39, 24 Sep 2026

The jump to $40M came the next day, and his reason was MIAX's market value:

> [!quote] "i need 40m signing bonus, if you see the perp futures announcement like the market dropped 15% on all exchange stocks … the stock will climb more than 40m in market cap on the announcement of the product." — Saheb, PREPACK v4.55, 25 Sep 2026

The room checked his claim and printed what the articles said, not 15%. On 2 June 2026, as markets absorbed the CFTC's approval of the first crypto perpetual, Cboe fell 9% in a session and CME, ICE and Nasdaq fell 4–5%. The Deal sets $40M beside MIAX's value: "about one percent of MIAX's market capitalization" (≈$3.9B in mid-September).

Three mechanics make the $40M Saheb's alone. The receivable is irrevocably assigned to him inside the master (¶5(h)). Line 2 of the Payment Direction pays it to his own account and cannot change without him. And the license takes effect only on his receipt in full (¶3(a)). The Tax Document's one rule: "The signing payment is the Founder's and the royalty is the Company's, and the two never mix."

> [!tension] AP's liability cap moved three times: "greater of the Signing Payment and 12 months" (v4.46), then all amounts paid for a title breach (v4.77), then three months' payments (v4.78). The prepack expects MIAX's counsel to raise the three-month cap.

## Fees: 0.63 basis points {#deal-fees}

The royalty is a share of fees MIAX sets, so the room proposes the fees as well. The Fee and Revenue Exhibit prices the pool and "deliberately" does not price the split. Fees stay "MIAX's alone" (¶5(g)).

**The launch schedule** is flat: $1.50 per contract per side, and $0.40 for a market maker. The realized contract is ≈$33,600 of notional (100 shares at the listing group's volume-weighted $335.82). At a 60/40 mix of regular and market-maker sides, a round turn collects ≈$2.12, or ≈0.63 basis points (bps, hundredths of a percent) of everything that trades.

> [!number] 0.63 bps — the launch fee pool as a share of traded notional
> ≈1.0 bp if MIAX adopts the optional tier schedule. Every $10B a day of trading ≈ $160M a year of pool at launch, ≈$250M at the tier schedule.

**The corridor.** The field runs from $0.35 to $10.00 a side. The recommended band, $1.25–$2.00, is where five tests overlap: above commoditized beta, a novelty premium, customer all-in at about half the offshore fee, the median name, and a market-maker rate near 24–30% of regular. The hard floor is $0.50 ($0.10 for market makers); the recommended ceiling $5.00; the hard ceiling $10.00.

**The optional tier schedule** steps the fee by share price and is re-bucketed every six months:

| Price bucket | Names | Regular / side | Market maker / side |
|---|---|---|---|
| Under $50 | 24 | $0.50 | $0.15 |
| $50–100 | 43 | $1.00 | $0.30 |
| $100–200 | 49 | $1.75 | $0.50 |
| $200–500 | 63 | $2.25 | $0.60 |
| $500+ | 21 | $2.75 | $0.75 |

The realized blend is ≈$1.73 a side, ≈1.0 bp. The Fee Exhibit calls that +63% over launch; the Investor Master says ≈+59%.

**Against the field.** All-in customer cost is ≈$2.07–$3.10 a side (≈0.83–1.24 bps) on a $250 name, against ≈4.5–5.5 bps for offshore base takers. The room's own multiple varies: "4–7× cheaper," "4–6× under" and "~5× under" all appear. CME's dated single-stock future charges $0.60 a side.

## Sixty percent against the benchmarks {#deal-benchmarks}

Sixty percent is far above what index licensors take, and the room says so openly. Its defense is that AP delivered the whole apparatus, not a name.

| Comparable | Disclosed | Effective take |
|---|---|---|
| Cboe–S&P (SPX options) | $233.3M royalty fees and other cost of revenues ÷ 1.03B index-option contracts (2024) | ≈$0.227 a contract, at most ≈25% of Cboe's ≈$0.905 revenue per contract |
| S&P DJI–CME (S&P 500 futures) | S&P DJI usage-based royalties $267M (2023 10-K) | ≈$0.18–0.21 a contract, ≈20–30% of CME's per-contract revenue (estimate) |
| SPY | 3 bps of ≈$800B AUM plus a fixed fee | ≈a third of the fund's 9.45 bp expense ratio |

> [!quote] "Index licensors take a fifth to a third — for a name. This delivers the apparatus." — MIAX Brief End to End p18, Sept 2026

The GLX-era research had triangulated lower, converging on "~25–35% of op profit ≈ ~15–25% of revenue," with a 50% ceiling at Hyperliquid's HIP-3. The two sets disagree on S&P DJI–CME: 20–30% in the Brief, ~15–19% in the GLX Fee Architecture work.

The case for 60% is what MIAX does not have to build: every filing drafted, the rulebook chapter, the 124-page specification, the OCC clearing plan, the corporate-actions apparatus and the street build. The case against it is MIAX's 40%, out of which MIAX bears every operating, regulatory and clearing cost.

> [!tension] 60% leaves MIAX 40% of the fees while the room's own benchmarks put licensors at 20–33%. This is the main negotiation risk, and no document records MIAX's view of it.

Saheb's own framing on 28 September was about durability, not the split: "Tell me how to make the contract the best paper. Really personally I don't care it's Miax / Like I'm only doing this thru/with them."

## What the stream is worth {#deal-valuation}

## Tax {#deal-tax}

The Tax Document covers two taxpayers and four money events, and its answer for Saheb is ordinary income throughout. The September view reverses the June memo, which had assumed capital gain.

**The four events.**

1. **IP Assignment.** No federal tax while the company is a disregarded single-member LLC; unpaid time creates no basis.
2. **The $40M.** Ordinary income in the year of the wire: a license, not a sale, of self-created work (§1221(a)(3)); no patent, so no §1235. Either self-employment tax (≈$1,420,000) or the 3.8% net investment income tax (≈$1,520,000) applies, never both. On wire day, set aside 37% federal + 3.8% + state and make estimated payments; it is reported in 1099-MISC box 2.
3. **The royalty.** On Saheb's return while the company is disregarded; once a buyer is admitted, investment income to the partners (Higgins), with 30% or treaty withholding for foreign members (§1441).
4. **The 10% sale.** Also ordinary (Rev. Rul. 99-5; Hort; P.G. Lake). The buyer amortizes over 15 years (§197).

> [!tension] The June memo told Saheb "roughly 23.8% federal plus your state" on a ~$210M capital gain. The September Tax Document says ordinary income on both the $40M and any unit sale.

**What the U.S. takes per $1 of a buyer's royalty** (Investor Master §09A): nothing from a U.S. tax-exempt; 0–15¢ from treaty investors (0¢ most of Europe, Japan, Canada; 15¢ Korea, India, Israel); 21¢ of the net from U.S. corporations and 41¢ from U.S. individuals, near zero for 15 years after §197 amortization at the ladder's multiples; 30¢ of the gross from non-treaty foreigners and sovereigns investing directly (§892 does not reach royalties). A royalty-note structure was rejected (§871(h)(4)).

**The §871(m) note.** §871(m) is the withholding rule for dividend equivalents paid to foreign holders, and a single-stock perpetual falls squarely under it. The product resolves this by construction: before each ex-date the exchange reports a per-share distribution amount, OCC lowers the prior settlement price by it, and that published figure is the withholding base ("base = factor × 100 × contracts"). Withholding becomes turnkey for the carrying firm. "It makes §871(m) clean" matters to the deal because foreign distribution is volume, and volume is royalty.

One tax point stays open for every carrying firm: how the hourly Funding Adjustment itself is treated.
